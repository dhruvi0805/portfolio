Wayfinding

Improving navigation experience for complex indoor environments

Role

UX Designer

Timeline

8 weeks

Platform

Mobile App

Executive Summary

A comprehensive wayfinding solution designed to help users navigate complex indoor environments like hospitals, airports, and large corporate buildings. The project focused on creating an intuitive interface that reduces navigation anxiety and improves user confidence in finding their destination.

Problem

Users frequently experience stress and anxiety when navigating unfamiliar large buildings. Traditional signage is often insufficient, leading to missed appointments, frustration, and negative experiences. The existing digital solutions were cluttered, confusing, and failed to account for accessibility needs.

Research

Conducted 24 user interviews with visitors, staff, and accessibility experts. Key findings included: 78% of users experienced navigation anxiety, particularly first-time visitors; accessibility features were consistently overlooked in existing solutions; users preferred step-by-step guidance over map-based interfaces.

Design Decisions

Based on research insights, we prioritized a step-by-step navigation approach with clear visual cues and accessibility features. The design emphasizes simplicity, with large touch targets and high contrast for visibility. We incorporated real-time updates and alternative route options to accommodate different user needs.

Solution

A mobile-first wayfinding application featuring intuitive turn-by-turn navigation, accessibility-first design, and real-time updates. The interface uses clear visual hierarchy, simple language, and progressive disclosure to prevent information overload. Key features include voice guidance, accessibility modes, and integration with building systems.

Impact

Post-launch testing showed a 45% improvement in navigation success rates, with users reporting significantly reduced anxiety levels. The accessibility features received positive feedback from users with diverse needs, and the solution was adopted across multiple facilities.

Reflection

This project reinforced the importance of accessibility-first design and the value of diverse user testing. The biggest challenge was balancing simplicity with comprehensive functionality. Future iterations could explore AR integration and more personalized routing options.
